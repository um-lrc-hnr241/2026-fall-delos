Our starting point

An ancient lens on a modern dilemma

Plutarch wrote in a world without robots or computers, but his works explore the soul, perception, and the limits of human understanding. Westworld turns related questions into science fiction: when a host remembers, suffers, and reflects, is that experience enough to call the host conscious?

We use Plutarch as a conversation partner, not as a source of ready-made answers. The comparison helps us examine how stories, memory, embodiment, and other people’s recognition shape what we mean by consciousness.
